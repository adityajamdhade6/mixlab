1/
I built a marketing mix model, then built a fake brand where I knew every channel's true ROI,
to see if the model could find it.

It mostly could. The interesting part is where it couldn't. 🧵

2/
Why bother: last-click attribution can't see sales that would have happened anyway.

In my synthetic brand it gave almost every channel an ROI near 3.5 and overstated Meta
five-fold. 59% of revenue was baseline that no ad earned.

3/
The model did better. True ROI fell inside its 94% range for 27 of 30 estimates, and it
predicted 13 unseen weeks within about 1.4%.

But the ranges are wide. Honest answer for most channels: "somewhere between poor and good."

4/
The surprise: the budget optimizer.

Capped at ±30% per channel, it promised +7.4% and truly delivered +3.3%.
Uncapped, it promised +16% and delivered zero.

Optimizers chase the model's overestimates. The boring guardrail was the valuable part.

5/
What fixes wide ranges isn't more modelling. One simulated 8-week lift test cut Google
Search's ROI range by 85%.
